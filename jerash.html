Jerash

Introduction

Jerash is one of the most important archaeological sites in Jordan. It is located in northern Jordan, about 45 kilometers north of Amman. The ancient city was known as Gerasa and has a history of human settlement going back more than 6,500 years.

History

Jerash reached its golden age during the Roman period, when it became one of the ten major cities of the Decapolis League. The city was an important center of trade and culture, and its Roman architecture remains remarkably well preserved.

Famous Landmarks

The archaeological site contains many impressive monuments, including the Oval Plaza, Hadrian's Arch, the Temple of Artemis, the Temple of Zeus, the South Theater, the Hippodrome, and the Colonnaded Street.

Jerash Today

Jerash was hidden under sand for centuries before archaeological excavations and restoration began. Today, visitors can walk through its ancient streets and explore its temples, theaters, plazas, and columns. The city also hosts the Jerash Festival for Culture and Arts.
